\section*{S-C. Further exploratory checks (Section V-M)}

\textbf{Label-using adaptations (Table S5).} The \textit{L2-SP warm start} keeps the FP16 heads and adds a correction fitted on the labeled Q4 vectors: the logits of head \(h\) become \(\ell_h(x_q) + \Delta W_h z + \Delta b_h\), where \(\ell_h\) are the FP16 logits of (2) and \(z\) is \(x_q\) standardized with the FP16 head's own means and deviations. The solver of (2) penalizes \(\frac{1}{2}\lVert \Delta W_h \rVert_F^2\), which is an L2 penalty toward the FP16 weights (L2-SP [57]) started at those weights; the intercept correction \(\Delta b_h\) is not penalized, so the strongest penalty leaves a bias-only correction. \(C\) is chosen from the same grid by the same three-fold masked-template cross-validation, on the labeled reports only. \textit{Map, then L2-SP fine-tuning} fits the same correction on mapped Q4 vectors (the map of Table 4 of the paper, fitted without labels), and \textit{map, then bias-only correction} refits only per-class offsets of the mapped logits, as in (S1) with \(\alpha_h = 1\). Bias-only correction and vector scaling of the FP16 logits are those of Section V-J.5. Every arm uses the label draws of the retraining arm: the five pre-specified draws and 15 further draws made with the same rule.

\textbf{Unlabeled budget (Table A1 of the paper).} Each map was fitted on the paired FP16 and Q4 vectors of \(n\) development fitting reports drawn at random (ten draws for each \(n\) below 1,000), with its ridge strength chosen by the same cross-validation inside those pairs; no fresh report was used. The FP16 heads are those of Table 4 of the paper. The lower rows of Table A1 of the paper (Section V-M.3 of the paper) use only reports that no head or map had seen: for new set-A reports, the 300 episodes were split at random into two halves of 150, and each half was adapted with a map fitted on the other half (all its 3,000 reports, or 100 or 300 of them in ten draws); for set B and the development style-B reports, one map was applied to all set-A reports. Pseudo-label retraining on such reports fits Q4 heads to the FP16 classifier's records on their FP16 vectors, with the code of the check confirmation.

\textbf{Field substitution (Table S6).} As in Section V-F of the paper, but in all eight combinations of the three fields. The Shapley share of a field is the average, over the orders in which the three fields can be replaced, of the misses removed when that field is replaced; the three shares add up to the misses removed by replacing all three.

\textbf{Robustness (Table A2 of the paper).} Each simulator variant changes one setting and replays the same records: two UAVs added near the base (UAV-04, RGB, 18 m/s; UAV-05, 16 m/s, RGB or RGB and THERMAL); deadlines multiplied by 0.75 or 1.5; arrivals in four bursts of five reports (mean gap 2 s inside a burst, longer gaps between bursts, so that an episode's expected length is that of the medium load); or bundles of one or five tasks per auction pass. For the second model family, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct [58] (the Qwen team's FP16 GGUF file, checked against its SHA-256; Q4\_K\_M made with llama-quantize of the same build, no importance matrix; 1,536-dimensional hidden vectors) was run with the same server settings, system prompt and user message in Qwen's chat format, at the \textit{prefix} and \textit{end} positions. Its heads, recentering, map and retrained heads were fitted on its vectors of the same 1,000 development reports, with the same code, folds, seeds and label draws; the fresh set-A reports, episodes and simulator are those of Table 4 of the paper.

\textbf{Recalibration comparators (Section V-J.5 of the paper).} We tested two cheap recalibrations that keep the FP16 classifier's weights and refit only its decision rule on Q4 vectors of the same labeled reports as the 100- and 300-label arms: per-class offsets added to the logits of each head (bias recalibration) and per-class scales plus offsets (vector scaling [56]). For head \(h\) with logits \(\ell_h(x) = W_h z + b_h\) (the rows of \(W_h\) are the \(w_{h,k}\) of (2) of the paper and the entries of \(b_h\) the \(b_{h,k}\)), vector scaling scores \(\alpha_h \odot \ell_h(x) + \beta_h\) with, over the \(m\) labeled reports,
\begin{equation}(\hat{\alpha}_h, \hat{\beta}_h) = \arg\min_{\alpha_h, \beta_h} \frac{1}{ m }\sum_{i=1}^m \mathrm{CE}_{h,i} + 0.01\, R_h,\label{eq:S1}\end{equation}
where \(\mathrm{CE}_{h,i}\) is the cross-entropy between the field label of report \(i\) and the scores \(\alpha_h \odot \ell_h(x_{q,i}) + \beta_h\) and \(R_h = \lVert \beta_h \rVert^2 + \lVert \alpha_h - 1 \rVert^2\); \(m\) is the number of labeled reports that carry the field of head \(h\) (for sensor and priority, only reports whose record creates or updates an incident); bias recalibration fixes \(\alpha_h = 1\). Both start from offsets of 0 and scales of 1 and are fitted with SciPy's L-BFGS-B routine (the bound-constrained variant of L-BFGS), with no bounds set; the penalty weight was set once, without tuning.

Tables S5 and S6 give details of Sections V-M.1 and V-M.4; Tables A1 and A2 of the manuscript those of Sections V-M.3, V-M.5 and V-M.7.
